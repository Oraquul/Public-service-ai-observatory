# Evidence levels E0 to E3

These labels are an editorial classification. They do not replace a formal evidence appraisal, a statistical review or an audit. Colour is never the only signal: the level is written out.

| Level | Meaning | What the journal may call it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E0 — Proposed | An idea or an intended pilot, without outcome evidence. Synthetic rehearsals stay here. | Design note or proposed pilot |
| E1 — Described | An authorised implementation account, or qualitative experience, with its limits stated. | Field note with limitations |
| E2 — Measured | A baseline and outcome measures, with the method and the limitations available. | Measured case. No causal claim without a suitable design |
| E3 — Replicated | A separate institution has tested an adaptation and provided measured results. | Replication evidence, with context and the limits that remain |

An independent reviewer checking an E2 case does not make it E3. A vendor’s statement remains an attributed claim until it is checked. Rerunning a synthetic script does not change the level.

Use this test before you assign a label:

- If there is no outcome evidence, the label is E0.
- If there is an authorised account and no measured outcome, the label is E1.
- If a baseline and an outcome were measured, the label may be E2. Association is not proof of cause.
- If a different institution has measured its own adaptation, the label may be E3, and even then the practice needs local approval.

The [export-enquiry rehearsal](/cases/case-ng-001/) is E0. Its minutes, correct-answer counts and naira totals are constructed fixtures. They can teach a comparison. They cannot be cited as a public-service result.

At least a quarter of the journal’s substantive pieces should examine limits, unsuccessful attempts or contested evidence. A clear E0 note is a valid publication. A successful-looking E2 label without a method is not.
