# Editorial policy

The founder and editor, Arc Benedict Itegbe, is accountable for what this site publishes. The rules below are the working policy from the operating playbook of 2 October 2026.

## What we publish

The journal publishes reviewed cases with replication packs, field notes, practical tools, governance explainers, interviews, the DBA Research Diary, and pointers to video. Core evidence cases and safety guidance stay free to read.

Material comes from public sources or from authorised, sanitised material. A contributor’s permission and an institution’s authority to release official material are different checks. Both have to be clear before release. The journal does not publish staff identifiers, personnel records or confidential documents, and the forms do not ask for them.

## Evidence and AI

Every case shows its evidence label. Proposed, described, measured and replicated work are not interchangeable. Synthetic data, a constructed fixture and a model-written draft are disclosed. AI output is a draft. A person approves a public release. A line in a form, or a sentence generated by a model, cannot mark itself approved.

The author of a case cannot be its only evidence reviewer. A material conflict means the person steps aside. Reviewers give an accept, revise or reject decision with reasons. The editor records the resolution. Evidence review here is an editorial process. It is not academic journal peer review.

Sensitive claims need both a subject review and a governance review. Decisions affecting rights, recruitment, benefits, enforcement, clinical care or surveillance are outside any launch-pilot deployment advice. An article may examine them. Deployment advice would need qualified expertise and formal institutional controls.

## Corrections

A substantive change after approval creates a new version. The [corrections](/corrections/) page explains how to ask for one, and it keeps the public log. If a claim is wrong, the canonical article is corrected and dependent notes are updated or marked stale. An editorial correction is not a substitute for an institution’s own incident process.

## Sponsors, credit and conduct

Sponsors cannot control findings, case selection or publication. Funding and relevant conflicts are disclosed. No sponsor is recorded on this site today. Recognition, if it is ever agreed, would be an acknowledgement of support, not an endorsement and not a logo placed here without permission.

Contributors are credited for the work they actually did, with their consent. An acknowledgement is not a qualification from the University of Oxford, the Aig-Imoukhuede Foundation or any employer. Personal views are distinguished from institutional statements.

Discussion is expected to be respectful and non-partisan. Contributors do not disclose confidential material, they declare financial interests, and they do not present a marketing pitch as a case. People may withdraw a contribution before publication on the agreed terms. Harassment or misrepresentation can lead to moderation and, if needed, removal through a documented process. Contributors should be free of workplace coercion.

## Research

A contribution to the blog or the Think Tank is not consent to doctoral research. The [DBA Research Diary](/diary/) is labelled so that a learning note is not mistaken for a finding.
