# Methodology

This page states the working method in the operating playbook of 2 October 2026. It is the standard a case on this site is written against. It is not a claim that every control already runs as a staffed production system.

## The six questions

A substantive case answers: what service problem was addressed; what changed; what evidence supports the result; what it cost; what could go wrong; and what another institution would need to repeat it. The [six-question worksheet](/tools/six-questions/) is the short form.

## From question to publication

1. **Define the question.** Give the case an identifier, a service problem, a reader, a decision to support, a jurisdiction, a risk and a possible non-AI alternative. Prefer “Can a source-linked assistant improve the accuracy and turnaround of routine enquiries?” to “How can AI transform government?”
2. **Search and log the search.** Use official Nigerian and African sources, government evaluations, peer-reviewed research and selected preprints. Record the query, the date, what was included and what was excluded. A weekly editorial scan is not a systematic review.
3. **Screen.** Keep a defined public-service problem with an identifiable source. Set aside unverifiable success claims, generic product launches and copied material with no origin. Check the date, the period, the place, the version, and whether a result was measured, projected or self-reported.
4. **Keep a claim ledger.** Each material claim needs a source, a location in that source, a date, an evidence type, a population, a denominator, units, limitations and a checker. Mark it verified, qualified, disputed or unsupported. Record disagreement. Do not average incompatible figures.
5. **Triangulate where the claim needs it.** One primary evaluation may support a narrow fact. Disputed or high-consequence claims need a second source. Ask what failed, who was left out, whether the result lasted, and what human labour was hidden.
6. **Compare.** Set the AI workflow beside the existing service and a simpler alternative. A before-and-after observation without a suitable comparator supports association, not causal proof.
7. **Draft from the ledger and review.** The author cannot be the only reviewer. Evidence and governance each need a sufficient score. Correct unsupported claims before approval.
8. **Follow up.** Recheck fast-changing tools and rules before publication. Revisit a measured case at 90 and 180 days. Revisit a template at least every six months, or when its model, policy or dependencies change.

## Evidence levels

| Level | Meaning | Permitted presentation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E0 — Proposed | Idea, intended pilot, or synthetic rehearsal, without outcome evidence | Design note or proposed pilot |
| E1 — Described | Authorised implementation account or qualitative experience | Field note with limitations |
| E2 — Measured | Baseline and outcome measures, with method and limitations | Measured case. No causal claim without a suitable design |
| E3 — Replicated | A separate institution tests an adaptation and provides measured results | Replication evidence, with context and remaining limitations |

An independent review of an E2 case does not make it E3. The full test is on the [evidence levels](/tools/evidence-levels/) page. The labels are written in words as well as codes.

A benchmark discussed in the playbook shows why context has to travel with a number. A UK cross-government Copilot trial of about 20,000 employees in late 2024 reported average time savings of 26 minutes a day. A later HMRC evaluation used a randomised cohort and reported more conservative self-reported savings of about 60 minutes a week. Those are different evaluations. Neither figure is a Nigerian result, and neither may be pasted onto a local service.

## Quality gate

A case or practical guide needs at least 80 out of 100 on the weighted rubric, with no dimension below 2 out of 4. Evidence and governance must each score at least 3. The weights in the playbook are: service relevance 10, evidence integrity 20, method and limitations 15, governance and ethics 15, replication usefulness 10, cost and maintainability 10, inclusion and citizen value 5, clarity and design 5, original contribution 5, transparency and freshness 5.

A critical defect blocks publication regardless of the total. Blocking defects include invented results, an unsupported consequential claim, missing release authority, a confidentiality breach, or a proposal presented as a proven success.

An E0 design note can pass if it gives a complete proposed evaluation and states, plainly, that outcome evidence is absent. The export-enquiry rehearsal records an illustrative 80 out of 100 on that basis. The score is a teaching exercise. It is not a certification, and the reviewers in that pack were simulated.

## Replication

A replication pack adds an operating procedure, a sanitised workflow, the prompt or rule, a data dictionary, a permissions checklist, an evaluation set, a cost worksheet, a training note, a risk and incident procedure, a rollback plan, and a 30/60/90-day pilot plan. It has a version, a maintainer and a next review date.

“Proposed workflow” means E0. “Pilot template” means an E1 or E2 method that still needs a local test. “Replicated practice” requires E3 and a current governance review. A complete-looking pack is not validated merely because it is complete. The [decision-boundary checklist](/tools/decision-boundary/) is one E0 extract.

## The research boundary

Blog contributions and Think Tank membership do not become doctoral research data. Recruitment for research needs ethics approval and a separate consent. The diary may discuss the question. It does not smuggle casual contributions into a dataset. The question the playbook poses, and does not answer, is how evidence standards, human review and peer learning influence responsible adoption and measured service outcomes in Nigerian public institutions.
