## What this journal is

The Public Service AI Observatory is a publication. The Public Service AI Think Tank is its contributor and learning community. *AI in Public Service with Benedict Itegbe* is the planned video series. The Public Service AI Brief is the newsletter. The DBA Research Diary is a labelled column about learning, questions and methods.

The mission is to document how public servants use AI and automation to improve service quality, accountability, inclusion and sustainable economic development, with sufficient evidence for others to make informed decisions. The core product is a reviewed case and a replication pack. Nigeria is the first centre of gravity. African and international examples are for comparison, not for copying a foreign productivity figure into a local budget.

The founder and editor is Arc Benedict Itegbe, an AIG Public Leaders Programme alumnus undertaking DBA studies with an Artificial Intelligence specialisation. That study explains the research interest. This site is not an accredited academic journal, and it is not part of an approved doctoral submission.

Fellow alumni of the AIG Public Leaders Programme are invited to opt in as contributors, together with other public servants, academics, engineers, privacy specialists and people who use public services. No other alumnus is named here. Membership of a forum does not give this site permission to republish conversations or member details.

## What it publishes

Every substantive case is written to answer six questions: what service problem was addressed, what changed, what evidence supports the result, what it cost, what could go wrong, and what another institution would need to repeat it.

Evidence is labelled. E0 is a proposal or a synthetic exercise. E1 is a described account. E2 is a measurement with its method. E3 is a replication by a separate institution. The first published case, [An export-enquiry assistant that knows when to stop](/cases/case-ng-001/), is E0. Its scenario is fictional. Its numbers are constructed fixtures.

At least a quarter of substantive pieces should examine limits, failures or contested evidence. A useful automation is not necessarily AI. Where a simpler process would do, the case should say so.

## The first 90 days, as a proposal

The concept note of 2 October 2026 proposes a 90-day founding pilot. The figures below are targets for that proposal. They are not results, and this site does not report them as achieved.

| Proposed part of the pilot | Proposed target, not a result |
| --- | --- |
| Written and video outputs | 10 to 12 written pieces, including six reviewed cases or field notes; six videos and twelve short clips |
| Community and audience | Ten active contributors; three online case clinics; 150 opt-in newsletter subscribers |
| Learning | One authorised institutional pilot started, if a suitable host and permissions are secured. No outcome is promised within 90 days |
| Review | A day-90 report on outputs, costs, quality, contributor experience and the conditions for expansion |

Days 1 to 30 would establish a founding circle, permissions, reader needs and the first briefs. Days 31 to 60 would develop cases and companion media. Days 61 to 90 would complete a first season and review the evidence. A proposed design will not be presented as a successful deployment.

## Planning envelope

The same concept note sets an indicative 90-day envelope of US$6,000: editorial coordination and research support 2,700; video production, captions and design 1,500; platform services, storage and automation 450; three online case clinics 600; privacy, accessibility and workflow review 500; contingency 250.

These are planning allocations, not supplier quotations, receipts or funds received. No sponsor is recorded on this site. No payment arrangement is offered here. Founder time, and some specialist review, were assumed in the note to be contributed. A sponsor cannot buy a finding, a case selection or a decision to publish. Support would not confer access to contributor data or an institutional endorsement.

## What is in place, and what is not

In place: this static journal, the operating method summarised on the [methodology](/methodology/) and [editorial policy](/editorial-policy/) pages, one labelled synthetic case, three proposed tools, and a diary note that states a question rather than a result.

Not in place, and not to be inferred from the design: a filled founding circle, a retained managing editor, a running case clinic, a sent newsletter, a recorded episode of *AI in Public Service*, an authorised institutional pilot, or a measured adaptation. The [Watch](/watch/) page links to an existing public playlist and says the series is coming soon.

## How to take part

Contributors opt in individually on the [Join the Think Tank](/join/) page. Readers who only want the briefing use the separate form on the homepage. Corrections, privacy requests and other messages go through [Contact](/contact/).
