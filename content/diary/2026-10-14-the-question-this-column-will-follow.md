# The question this column will follow

This column will keep a dated record of learning, methods and mistakes. It is not a results series, and this note is not a finding.

In the operating playbook of 2 October 2026 the editor set down a possible doctoral question: how do evidence standards, human review and peer learning influence responsible adoption and measured service outcomes in Nigerian public institutions?

That sentence is a direction for study. The blog cannot answer it. A feasible approach, also only a proposal in the playbook, would be a longitudinal multiple-case study, with purposive variation across service, institutional level and maturity, including teams that did not adopt a tool and cases that failed. Indicators would need to be defined in advance. None of that work is reported here.

Formal research would need a supervisor’s agreement, university ethics approval, and consent that is separate from a message sent to this site. Casual conversation with colleagues is not a consented interview. The publication is not an accredited journal, and it is not part of an approved doctoral submission.

The first case, [An export-enquiry assistant that knows when to stop](/cases/case-ng-001/), is a reminder of the boundary. Its figures are synthetic. They can show why a knowledge-base comparison and a reviewer’s veto belong in the method. They cannot be upgraded from E0 to E2 because a script ran, and they cannot be cited as evidence that a service improved.

Further notes will be added when there is a method, a mistake or a source worth recording. Proposed targets for the journal are not entries in this diary.
